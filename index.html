Who Holds
the Pen? Executive orders, gridlock & American democracy, 1991–2025

When democracies backslide, scholars argue over which institution gave way first: an overreaching executive or a stalled legislature. This project builds a new measure of how strongly Congress constrains the president and takes a first test: which one tracks the decline in U.S. democracy?

Building the CISI

The Congressional Institutional Strength Index (CISI) will measure how strongly Congress constrains the president across five components. This preliminary test used two of them.

Verdict: the executive

These preliminary results favor executive aggrandizement over legislative gridlock as the more salient mechanism of democratic erosion in the contemporary U.S.

Data. Liberal democracy: V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index (v2x_libdem), United States, last observation per year, 1991–2025. Executive orders: Federal Register executive order records, counted by signing year. Bills enacted: approximate enacted bills per Congress (rounded enacted share × total bills and resolutions), assigned to both years of each Congress, 102nd–118th. Public trust: share who trust the federal government to do what is right “just about always” or “most of the time,” compiled by Pew Research Center from Pew, NES, Gallup, CBS/NYT, ABC/Washington Post and CNN polls, 1958–2025.

Method. OLS with Newey-West (HAC) standard errors, 3 lags, 1991–2024 (n = 34). Executive orders and bills are entered as z-scores. Two components entered separately: R² = 0.243, F-test p = 0.004; executive orders β = −0.082 (p = 0.002); bills β = 0.002 (p = 0.809). Equal-weight composite: R² = 0.014, not significant. Public trust alone (n = 33): R² = 0.002, p = 0.847. 2025 is shown for context only; it is not in the two-component model because the 119th Congress is not finished.

Limitations. The sample is small (34 years), residual autocorrelation remains (Durbin-Watson ≈ 0.54), and the productivity measure is estimated from rounded percentages rather than exact counts. Correlation across years is not proof that executive orders cause democratic decline.
